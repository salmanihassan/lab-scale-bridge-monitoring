1 Introduction
The REPS Digital Twin project uses a laboratory-scale instrumented bridge to investigate how measured
response varies with applied mass, loading position, commanded vibration, temperature, and external
disturbances. The present work establishes a reproducible experimental visualization baseline for the
Department of Transportation progress presentation. It supports assessment of data coverage and
limitations before model development. The analysis prioritizes comparable acquisition conditions and
transparent provenance. Repeated runs summarize reproducibility at the recording level; individual
time samples are not treated as independent experimental replicates. Numerical ﬁndings are generated
directly from retained cleaned data.
2 Experimental System and Instrumentation
The cleaned schema provides three acceleration axes in units of g, three temperature channels in degrees
Celsius, two load-cell raw-output channels, a timestamp in seconds, and a logged motor state. The
channel names identify left, center and right temperature locations and left and right load cells. The
current ﬁles do not establish bridge geometry, exact accelerometer orientation, traceable calibration
factors, or sensor uncertainty; these details are therefore not invented. The existing processing script
maps applied-mass codes L01, L02 and L03 to 0.25, 0.50 and 1.00 kg. It maps left, center and right
loading positions to normalized labels 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75, and vibration codes V01, V02 and V03
to PWM commands 64, 128 and 192. These are experimental metadata, not values estimated from
sensor outputs. P00 does not establish a physical loading coordinate. Load-cell outputs are reported in
uncalibrated counts. Diﬀerences and normalized ratios between these channels are signal descriptors and
cannot establish support forces or load-sharing fractions without calibration. PWM is never converted
into physical excitation frequency. Filename F labels are retained as commanded-frequency metadata
only.

RMS is sample weighted over all ﬁnite residuals, including both logged motor states. A motor OFF
sample within a switching sequence is not assumed to be an undisturbed baseline. Missing samples
remain missing and no extra peak ﬁltering is applied. Time-history lines are broken at gaps larger than
1.5 times the median positive timestamp increment. Static aggregates use run medians to reduce the
inﬂuence of retained extreme values. Error bars, where shown, are sample standard deviations between
matching runs and are not conﬁdence intervals.
Representative selection and comparability
Representative recordings are selected deterministically as the run nearest the median relevant metric
within each displayed group, with relative ﬁlename ordering to break ties. Aggregate comparisons retain
acquisition batch, mass, position, PWM, frequency label and disturbance status as applicable. Missing
metadata remain unknown. Temperature is not treated as a controlled covariate.

7 Frequency-Domain Analysis
Timing is audited before spectral analysis. The cleaner converted milliseconds to seconds and had
already removed nonincreasing timestamps or selected a reset segment. The present cleaned timestamps
can therefore reveal remaining jitter and gaps but cannot reconstruct discarded raw resets. The recurring
increments near 0.09–0.10 s, in otherwise approximately 0.01 s sampling, are treated conservatively as
missing acquisition intervals. Their acquisition cause cannot be established from cleaned data alone.
For each run, the algorithm splits at missing acceleration, nonincreasing timestamps, or a timestamp
increment greater than 1.5 times the median. It selects the longest remaining interval and requires at
least four seconds, at least 256 samples, and maximum interval deviation no greater than 20 percent
of the local median. Four seconds gives at least two cycles at the lowest inspected 0.5 Hz and a
nominal Fourier spacing no coarser than 0.25 Hz. These are minimum engineering screening rules, not
a certiﬁcation of stationarity or anti-aliasing. Only an accepted interval may be linearly interpolated
onto its median-increment grid, without extrapolation or bridging rejected gaps. The implementation
then uses linear detrending, a periodic Hann window with coherent-gain amplitude normalization, and
MATLAB Signal Processing Toolbox functions for Welch PSD and spectrograms. Welch uses two-
second windows with 50 percent overlap. The searched response-peak band is 0.5 Hz to the smaller
of 40 Hz and 0.4 times the sampling rate. The output is a response spectrum, not a transfer function
because input force is not measured. Result for this execution: no spectral analysis is emitted.
FFT amplitude spectra, Welch PSD, spectrograms, dominant-frequency comparisons, frequency-versus-
load plots and commanded-versus-measured peak plots are omitted. Timing diagnostics are included
instead. No peaks are inferred from PWM or from the approximately 100 Hz median-interval sampling
rate. T0236 does meet the timing screen: its longest continuous interval is 7.399 s, at 100 Hz, from
13.106 to 20.505 s. Analysis of this interval is withheld because the required Signal Processing Toolbox
functions are not installed. The other recordings fail the timing-duration screen.

9 Temperature-Induced Sensor Drift
THERMAL_01 consists of two recording segments from one session with an approximately ﬁve-minute
interruption. Each segment has its own elapsed-time axis, starting at its ﬁrst ﬁnite sensor record. No
samples are invented and no line crosses the interruption. Part 2 contains an all-missing leading record
followed by a 539.826 s timestamp gap; this artifact is distinct from the approximate interruption
supplied in the experiment description. Thermal scatter and drift summaries use occupied one-second
median bins, explicitly as summaries rather than a replacement cleaned dataset. Empty bins are not
ﬁlled. For each sensor, a trend is ﬁtted to paired ﬁnite bin summaries only when there are at least 20
bins and a temperature span of at least one degree Celsius. Fits use left temperature as the common
measured reference and are computed separately by segment. The reported slopes and coeﬃcients of
determination describe observational associations. Temporal autocorrelation, temperature gradients,
possible orientation changes, excitation and segment oﬀsets prevent interpreting the ﬁts as a validated
compensation model.

12 Data Limitations and Future Work
All retained recordings have source QC warnings. Near-complete ﬁnite sensor coverage must not be
mistaken for trustworthy timing or calibrated physical measurement. The following limitations should
guide the next experimental campaign.
• Acquire hardware-timestamped data with veriﬁed units, a measured sampling clock, gap logging
and an anti-aliasing strategy. Record suﬃciently long uninterrupted vibration intervals.
• Establish independent load-cell calibration, tare checks, sensor orientation and measurement uncer-
tainty. Retained extreme values require review of acquisition and earlier cleaning before calibration
or safety-related inference.
• Preserve raw data and sample-level cleaning provenance. The current cleaned ﬁles do not identify
which values were previously interpolated or spike-ﬁltered. The present pipeline cannot reconstruct
lost peaks.
• Collect same-batch unloaded and loaded baselines, balanced repetitions and recorded tempera-
tures. Use a tachometer or measured excitation channel to distinguish motor command from
actual excitation.
• Record disturbance triggers, wind speed and impact force before attempting damping, settling-
time or frequency-response identiﬁcation.
• Repeat controlled thermal cycles with ﬁxed load and excitation to test compensation on indepen-
dent sessions. The two present thermal segments are one session, not a validation split.
• Resolve duplicate and suspicious test identiﬁers using acquisition logs. Current traceability uses
complete relative paths, preserving the existing identiﬁers.
The cleaned load-cell data retain 115 entries at or above an absolute magnitude of 8,388,607 counts in 4
recordings. The largest retained left-channel value is 7.97700158e+12 counts. This threshold is an audit
ﬂag motivated by the project HX711 acquisition code, not an automatic correction or a new source QC
decision. Median summaries reduce the inﬂuence of isolated extreme entries on descriptive aggregates.
13 Conclusion
The automated pipeline produces a traceable experimental characterization package from the retained
cleaned bridge recordings. It documents static and position-dependent outputs, vibration response under
recorded commands, thermal associations and disturbance signals while preserving source data. Same-
batch comparisons provide a basis for planning improved experiments, but current timing and calibration
limitations prevent defensible modal claims or validation of a digital twin. The next phase should resolve
acquisition continuity and measurement calibration before model training and independent validation.
